01 · Interactive 3D site

Aero Atlas

How Formula 1's rules shaped the cars, from the 1961 1.5-litre formula to the 2026 active-aero cars, in one interactive 3D model.

  • three.js
  • JavaScript
  • Procedural 3D geometry
  • Data research
66seasons modelled
919team-seasons in the grid data
25radical designs explained
  • Builds a regulation car for every season from that year's FIA figures (width, wing spans and heights, overhangs, wheelbase, tyres, weight), with a cited source on every number and both values shown where sources disagree.
  • All geometry is generated in code: lofted chassis and sidepod sections, NACA-style aerofoils, lathed tyres, and era-specific details such as skirts, grooved tyres, the halo and 2026 movable wings.
  • "Legal box" view of the space the rules allow, an illustrative airflow view, era comparison as a ghost overlay, and every season's grid from the open F1DB dataset.

02 · Full-stack web app

Pitwall

An F1 telemetry lab: compare any drivers' laps from real timing data, replay them in 3D, and run a live strategy pit wall.

  • Python
  • FastAPI
  • FastF1
  • OpenF1
  • Plotly
  • three.js
53automated tests
4drivers compared at once
6analysis tabs
  • Lap comparison from official timing data: speed, throttle, brake and gear traces, lap-time delta, track dominance, braking zones and corner-by-corner analysis. The fastest lap is always the reference.
  • Pit wall strategy engine: fuel-corrected tyre-degradation fits, undercut and overcut calls, rejoin position, pit window, safety car and VSC time loss, and an ERS estimate.
  • 3D lap replay with chase, onboard and TV cameras; race replays from past data, and support for a live OpenF1 feed within its rate limits.

Runs on a Python server, so it is shown here with screenshots.

03 · Simulation

Hybrid Energy-Management Simulator

How should an engine and an electric motor share the load? A simulator that tests energy-management strategies against the best possible one.

  • Python
  • NumPy
  • Dynamic programming
  • ECMS
0.3–4.7%ECMS gap to the optimum
15.8%fuel saved on a mixed cycle
26physics & regression tests
  • Quasi-static model of a parallel turbo-hybrid: drag, rolling resistance, Willans-line engine, MGU-K, battery equivalent circuit and an MGU-H.
  • Compares rule-based control, ECMS and a dynamic-programming benchmark priced fairly on stored energy. ECMS captures 92–100% of the achievable hybrid saving.
  • Checked the class report's claims: 280 km/h and 500 km range are two different operating points, and 500 km holds only up to about 176 km/h steady.

04 · Engineering design

Petrol Turbo-Hybrid Powertrain

A class project to design a vehicle powertrain, taken from concept to CAD model and scale build.

  • CAD
  • Vehicle dynamics
  • Technical report
  • Ruled out an early self-charging wind-turbine concept on conservation of energy: pulling energy from the car's own airflow costs more drag than it recovers.
  • Sized power at speed from the drag power equation P = ½ρCdAv³, then iterated through combustion, battery-electric and hybrid layouts.
  • Settled on a petrol turbo-hybrid as the best balance of range, performance and feasibility, then built the simulator above to test the report's numbers.
